# Model and training choices for the cancer research platform

## Recommendation

Start with **Google Gemma 4 31B IT as the proposed large-machine research coordinator**, **a smaller quantized Gemma 4 for local workflow development**, **MedSigLIP embeddings plus a small supervised classifier as the first bone-imaging baseline**, and **MedGemma 1.5 4B as a separate medical vision-language comparison**. Benchmark NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super or Bolt against the coordinator if efficiency matters. These are proposed experiments, not measured winners on the project.

Use the GB300 first to establish reproducible data preparation, specialist evaluation, and modest adaptation. Large frontier models can help with literature, code, experiment planning, and structured tool calls; their general benchmark scores do not establish cancer-detection validity. No model was downloaded, served, trained, or uploaded during this research.

The strongest practical choice depends on **correct task completion per GPU-hour**, not just tokens/second or active parameter count. Measure latency, peak memory, total input/output tokens, retries, invalid tool calls, unsupported-model selection, and successful completion on the same test cases. A short incorrect answer is not efficient.

### Memory planning

For Gemma 4, use [Google's published model-loading estimates](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core): about **69.9 GB for 31B BF16**, **17.5 GB for 31B Q4_0**, **6.7 GB for 12B Q4_0**, and **4.5 GB for E4B Q4_0**. These exclude additional context and serving memory. [NVIDIA lists 16 GB VRAM for a desktop RTX 5070 Ti](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/graphics-cards/50-series/rtx-5070-family/), so 31B Q4_0 will not fit fully in its VRAM; the smaller variants are plausible local inference candidates, not measured runs. Training adds further state. A checkpoint's actual format and supported runtime take precedence over rough arithmetic.

Likewise, a 552B model is not a 16B-sized download because only 16B parameters participate in decoding. Total weights still need storage and access. “Flash” is not a guarantee of a small single-GPU footprint.

Earlier NPS inspection verified a 30G `testspace-30g` PVC using `local-path` and readWriteOnce, plus other named sources whose capacities were not inspected. That 30G request is smaller than the 31B BF16 model-loading estimate. Arrange an appropriate model cache, dataset volume, and checkpoint space before downloading. NPS GPU model/memory, topology, job-specific quota, runtime compatibility, and usable free capacity remain unverified. No serving recipe in this document is an NPS-tested launch command.

## Medical models and dataset candidates

[MedSigLIP](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medsiglip) is particularly relevant to efficiency: Google documents training a small classifier over cached image embeddings. Its image encoder has 400M parameters; a separate text encoder has 400M. The documented image resolution is 448×448. This is an economical classification/retrieval baseline, **not a proven bone-tumor specialist**.

[MedGemma 1.5 4B](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card) is the medical vision-language alternative when text generation is needed. Google documents CT/MRI volume representations, whole-slide patch input, longitudinal chest X-rays, and other medical tasks. Medical pretraining does not establish performance on the project's bone task. The Health AI Developer Foundations terms apply; no terms were accepted or gated weights fetched here.

## Architecture for the aggregator

Implement compatibility as a **registry and validation rule**, with the language model assisting the interface. Record each specialist's modality, anatomy, input dimensionality, preprocessing, expected spacing/resolution, label ontology, intended task, output schema, supported population, version, license, and validation evidence.

Use DICOM metadata where present, supported by image checks or a small modality/anatomy classifier when necessary. The backend should reject unsupported or conflicting inputs. JPEGs from research datasets may not preserve DICOM metadata. A language model can propose a route; the registry must determine whether the route is permitted. Explicitly support unknown anatomy/modality, image-quality failures, and no eligible specialist.

Keep outputs separate unless a fusion model has been trained and evaluated on appropriately paired examples. Do not average a bone X-ray malignancy probability with a histology viability probability or a genomic score: they describe different inputs and endpoints.

For the dataset catalog, record original source, release/DOI, license and required citation, patient/study/series identifiers when available, modality, anatomy, labels and reference standard, image/annotation pairing, checksum and duplicate group, split membership, access restrictions, preprocessing, and exclusions. These fields are required research provenance, not optional authoring metadata.

## Training advice and experiment order

1. **Freeze the scientific task.** For the bone branch, define the exact normal/benign/malignant labels after checking the dataset. An initial tumor-versus-normal task can be easier to debug, but it must not be reported as benign-versus-malignant detection. Do not mix CT, X-ray, and histology under a shared cancer label.
2. **Audit and split before augmentation.** Group by patient/study and duplicate family; reserve an untouched test set. Hold out a center or source when the data support it. If patient IDs are unavailable, report that limitation: duplicate grouping and center separation cannot prove patient independence. Inspect text markers, acquisition artifacts, and source differences as potential shortcuts.
3. **Run the cheapest useful baseline.** Cache frozen MedSigLIP image embeddings and train a regularized small classifier. Compare it with one conventional pretrained image classifier under identical splits. Use the validation set for model selection and operating thresholds. This is likely a more useful first experiment than fine-tuning a giant coordinator.
4. **Adapt only after baseline evidence.** Try limited encoder fine-tuning or an adapter, with early stopping and validation-selected settings. Use class weighting or balanced sampling only within training. Compare full-image and ROI-assisted approaches carefully: if evaluation uses annotated boxes, label it as such; an automatic deployment pipeline needs an actual detector, not ground-truth boxes supplied at inference.
5. **Evaluate specialist and router separately, then together.** Measure malignant sensitivity, specificity, per-class precision/recall, confusion matrix, PR-AUC/ROC-AUC, calibration, and uncertainty intervals at the correct independent unit. Measure route errors and abstentions too. Overall accuracy can hide failure on the clinically important minority class. Grad-CAM or fluent explanations are not validation.
6. **Keep the coordinator pretrained initially.** Use retrieval over the model registry, dataset catalog, and approved papers; return source-linked structured results. Fine-tune only for measured schema/tool/workflow failures. Create reviewed examples of valid routing, incompatible-input rejection, abstention, result explanation, and provenance extraction; keep a separate held-out evaluation set. Do not teach it synthetic cancer labels as ground truth.
7. **Use LoRA/QLoRA selectively.** [PEFT documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/peft/developer_guides/quantization) explains low-bit base weights with trainable adapters. This reduces adaptation memory; it does not automatically validate every architecture, quantization format, GPU kernel, or ARM container. For Gemma or MedGemma, confirm the exact supported recipe first. Keep the vision encoder frozen initially if the task permits. Do not use an inference-only GGUF artifact as a generic training checkpoint.
8. **Optimize after validation.** Compare BF16 with the proposed quantized export on the same held-out cases, including calibration and minority-class recall. Benchmark batch size, context budget, latency, memory, and end-to-end completion. For Jetson, choose a genuinely smaller deployable component and test it on the actual device; neither a 24GB target nor a GB300 result proves a fit on a 16GB Jetson.

Do not start frontier pretraining, large-scale RL, or a multi-organ model from scratch for the first weekend. The immediate experiment should answer a narrow question that the existing dataset can support. The resulting system remains a research prototype until appropriate external and expert validation supports broader claims.

## A practical weekend target

The proposed deliverable is one reproducible bone-X-ray experiment, a curated source manifest, and an aggregator demonstration that selects compatible registered specialists and records their results. Sequence the work as dataset/label audit → grouped split → frozen-embedding baseline → one matched comparison → evaluation/error review → registry integration. Add coordinator fine-tuning only if it solves a demonstrated problem.

Once execution is authorized, profile one small batch and short run before choosing a GPU request or estimating runtime. The earlier NPS presets' tiny CPU/RAM requests are not training recommendations. Confirm mounted persistent storage and save checkpoints there.
